# Browsing and ordering — working session, transcribed verbatim

> Search is the front door. Someone types two or three letters and wants to see
> what we sell. If they type one letter that is not a search, that is a browse,
> and we do not have a browse.

> Show out-of-stock things. Hiding them makes people think we do not carry the
> item at all, and then they buy it somewhere else. Say it is out of stock and
> let them decide.

> Never send back a page bigger than fifty items. Mobile chokes and someone will
> eventually ask for ten thousand.

> Placing an order: they pick a thing, say how many, and we tell them the total.
> We compute the total. The client does not get to tell us what something costs
> any more than it gets to tell us what a refund is.

> If the same order arrives twice we must not create two. The app retries on a
> flaky connection and that is our problem, not the customer's — they send us a
> key with the request and we honour it.

> We do not take orders for things we cannot ship. If it is out of stock at the
> moment they press the button, that is a conflict, and the message should say
> which item.

> Every order that gets placed is written down the same way a cancellation is.
> Same reasons, same retention.
