# Cancellation — working session, transcribed verbatim

> A customer should be able to cancel an order themselves as long as we
> haven't shipped it. Once it's with the carrier it's a returns problem, not
> a cancellation problem, and those are different teams.

> When they cancel we stop fulfilment. Warehouse has 5 seconds to see it —
> after that a picker may already have the item in hand and we're paying for
> a return.

> Every cancellation gets written down. Who did it, when, which order, why.
> That's an audit thing, not a logging thing — compliance asked for 90 days
> retrievable and they will ask for it.

> Refunds are billing's problem. We tell them and they settle it. If they
> come back and say it's settled, we record that too.

> One thing that bit us before: the client was sending us the refund amount
> in the cancel request. Don't ever trust that. We work it out.

> Access tokens carry the user id and the signing key id. They must not
> carry an issuer claim — we had a bug where two environments both trusted
> the other's tokens because the issuer was blank and nobody checked.
