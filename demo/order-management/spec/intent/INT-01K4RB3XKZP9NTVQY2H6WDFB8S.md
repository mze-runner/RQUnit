# Delivery — working session, transcribed verbatim

> The carrier tells us when it lands. They post to us; we do not poll them. It
> is signed, and if the signature does not check out we did not hear anything.

> Three outcomes we care about: delivered, refused at the door, and address not
> found. Anything else the carrier invents, we do not accept.

> Once it is delivered it is over. No cancelling a delivered order — that is a
> return, and returns are a different team with a different budget.

> A failed attempt is not the end. They try again. We only close the order when
> it is delivered or when it comes back to us.

> Customers ask "where is my order" constantly. Whatever we know about the
> delivery has to be visible on the order itself, not buried in a log somewhere.

> Same audit rule as everything else: when the state changes, write it down.
> The carrier's attempt count goes in it — that is the first thing anyone asks
> when a delivery goes wrong.
